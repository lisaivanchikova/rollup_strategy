Industry

Of the eight back-office families that survived the economy-wide screen, MSP is the only one where contracted recurring revenue converts into a published valuation multiple.

Everything below is graded against one quantity: founder cash-out at exit — multiple × scale × retained ownership, weighted by the probability of an exit on horizon.

Two practitioners converge on the same line

Kevin Damghani, seven deals, IT Partners: target profile is 60%+ recurring revenue and $150–850K EBITDA — “generally under a million, because PE gets excited about a million or more.”

Steve, thirty deals, peer-group operator: “I would go even smaller, because I want to be below the radar of all the PE money.” The line sits near $500K. The reason is arithmetic — “I want companies we can get at five or six times, not the ones we have to take to seven, eight, nine to compete with another buyer.”

The first deal is the exception

It has to be larger, to give the platform a footprint the smaller ones can be folded into: revenue $2–3M, net profit 15–20%, price $3–3.5M, several consecutive years of that profit. Fastest close on record from the same source: 90 days; with cash ready and counsel in place, a month is possible.

Two contract clauses decide the multiple. Agreements must be assignable — without that clause a sale falls to state law, which differs everywhere. And a three-year agreement with a 90-day exit is a month-to-month agreement: “your business is worth about half the multiple you think it is.” One-year terms instead of three cost roughly 1.5× of multiple.
Waiting is the most expensive option on the table. A live case from the buyer’s side: $2M offered in the autumn; the owner waited and lost three key contracts; the January offer was $1.1M, and it closed at that. Six months of hesitation cost nearly a million dollars.
