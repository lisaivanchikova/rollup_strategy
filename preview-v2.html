Node 01

Industry

Of the eight back-office families that survived the economy-wide screen, MSP is the only one where recurring revenue converts into a published valuation multiple.

The whole tree is graded against one quantity: founder cash-out at exit — multiple × scale × retained ownership, weighted by the probability of an exit on horizon. Every node below narrows the field against that number.

The screen ran across 1,012 six-digit NAICS cells and produced 22 hell-yes verticals, clustering into eight back-office families: accounting, IT / MSP, HR, legal back-office, marketing, translation, collections, telemarketing. Industry filter: fragmentation ≥ 80%, recurring revenue ≥ 50%, EBITDA margin ≥ 18%.

SelectedMSP — decision of 17 July 2026. Home Care stepped down to a listening post; HR and accounting closed.
Why it winsMSP is the one family with a public, buyer-side scorecard: contracted recurring revenue maps directly onto a known multiple range. The others price on negotiation, not on a table.
Price of the cutNo vertical diversification before year three. The bet is concentrated by design.

Why A wins

The value is created by the recurring leg, not the software one. Software brings $5M, but it is project revenue — in his own words, “most of it is project based, and I want recurring revenue.” Project revenue is priced on a services scorecard, at a discount. Managed-services revenue lands on the 584 & Cogent scale, where the ceiling is 8–10× and, at $40M+ scale, the market has paid 15–16×. The multiple expansion comes from the leg we barely have.

A product cannot exist before the channel does. Jeff McHugh put the sequence plainly: automate one critical, regulator-mandated workflow for one MSP in a regulated industry, and only then does that automation become a product you sell onward — through the RMM vendors, not directly to the MSPs you are also buying.

“First you buy the client base, then you build software on top of it… I don’t believe in building software and then running around trying to push it onto every MSP — that just turns us back into SaaS.” — Nicolas, 4 August

The revenue mix has to move toward recurring anyway. Damghani: the highest multiples go to an 80% MRR / 20% hardware-and-projects mix on three-year agreements with an assignability clause. Today the group sits on the inverse mix.

Why not B

There is nothing to separate yet. A line of business turning over $150K is a pilot, not an asset; splitting it out buys two sets of accounts and two teams while a single leg carries the group. The claim that software would fetch more on its own rests on no source in the record — software multiples were not discussed at ChannelCon at all.

Why not C

It removes the recurring revenue the MSP was started to create, and leaves the group inside the project-based model it is trying to leave.

What the choice rests on. A single assumption: that the channel can in fact be built. This is the weakest joint in the tree — the MSP today is seven people and one anchor client at 25 seats, while phase B of the roadmap builds a billion-dollar outcome on a client base that does not exist yet.
Trigger to revisitExternal software sales start to require the trust of MSPs we are not acquiring · the fund demands separate perimeters for separate assets · twelve months pass without a first platform deal.
