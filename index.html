Page 1 · Where to play

Seven filters cut 40,000 US providers to a pool in the low hundreds — and only the first two rest on published data

An MSP runs another company's IT. Its «vertical» is the industry its clients are in — a financial-services MSP serves banks, brokers and advisers; a healthcare MSP serves clinics and dental practices. We are buying IT firms, and the vertical is the answer to whose IT the firm we buy already runs. Fifty-eight per cent of US providers now concentrate on at least one; in 2021 it was thirty-nine.

Which verticals exist inside MSP, and what happened to each

Twenty slices were scored on acquirer-side criteria — target availability, client economics, compliance moat, how compressible the work is, room, entry price, exit premium and our own edge. Three passed: broker-dealers and RIAs (6.96), banks and credit unions (6.74), healthcare (6.56). Defence contractors under the CMMC mandate missed by 0.04 and are the most time-sensitive slice on the board — a federal deadline of 10 November 2026 against a supplier base of 350,000 with roughly 1% ready.

Why specialise at all

Vertical specialists price at $250–350 per seat against $100–120 and carry roughly 23% more margin. Generalists score lowest of twenty slices screened, on the largest pool and cheapest entry — no moat cannot be offset. The single-service route caps out: the database-only operator reached $750K of revenue after three decades.

Why this vertical, and the honest caveat

What is sold is an evidentiary obligation — FINRA examinations and GLBA audits demand documentation a generic provider cannot produce. Healthcare scored within a rounding error and is larger, but is the most consolidated slice in the market. What selects ours is transferable domain knowledge that has never been tested here.

Five of seven filters are estimates and they compound: the pool could be seventy firms or three hundred. Hardening it is a data purchase, not analysis — the cheapest open item in the programme.

Page 2 · How to enter

Half the competitive field is unreachable — not on nerve, but on what you must already own to stand there

The correction that moved the picture

The middle of this scale was read as «assess, keep the strengths, absorb the weak functions». That describes the owner's job, not the firm's operations: ITPartners+ runs one team, one coat of paint from day one and says so from the stage. There is no hidden centralising turn at year five — and the scale has two live positions, not three.

Why rebuild is sequenced, not rejected

Over 70% of integrations miss the synergies modelled at close, and private-equity deals with more than two add-ons returned 19.9% against 23.1% for standalone buyouts. Integration capacity, not deal sourcing, is the constraint — so the platform gets built on our own operation and the first purchase. From deal two, migration onto it is the integration.

The decision. Buy one financial-services MSP at $3–4M of revenue and 5–6× adjusted EBITDA, standardise it onto a single platform, then twelve bolt-ons. A larger anchor is not a judgement call — at $10M of revenue it costs $18M, sixty per cent of the budget for a quarter of the target. We cannot buy a platform; we can only assemble one.

What we are asking for is one signed LOI — not a round, not a partner, not another quarter of research. It prices our client-acquisition cost, tests the integration claim, gives a lender a borrowing base, and shows whether small firms transact at 4–5× or at 6×. The kill criterion is equally small: a hundred contacts and no LOI across two quarters means sellers will not transact at our price, and it is the entry model that failed rather than the vertical.

Three things this does not establish: that we can integrate, which the outside record says is what kills roll-ups; demand for the software layer beyond our own portfolio; and the vertical on market data rather than on a claim about ourselves.
